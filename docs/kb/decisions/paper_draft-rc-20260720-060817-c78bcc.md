# Stage 20: Paper Draft

## UCI-HAR Baseline Audit: Reproducibility of Linear SGD and Random Forest under Strict Subject Splits

## Abstract
Establishing reliable baselines in Human Activity Recognition (HAR) requires strict adherence to dataset protocols, yet many studies obscure subject-level leakage or lack rigorous statistical validation. Existing literature often fails to report subject-independent split compliance, leading to ambiguous performance claims for lightweight models on the UCI-HAR benchmark. We present a reproducibility audit of two canonical baselines: Linear Stochastic Gradient Descent (LogLoss) and Random Forest (200 estimators). The study enforces the official subject-based train/test split, utilizes three independent random seeds, and reports Accuracy and Macro-F1 with 95% confidence intervals. Linear SGD achieved a mean Macro-F1 of **0.937**, slightly outperforming Random Forest at 0.928. However, paired statistical tests yielded conflicting significance levels (parametric $p=0.007$ vs. non-parametric $p=0.25$) due to limited sample size. We conclude that while Linear SGD demonstrates competitive performance, definitive superiority claims require expanded sampling to resolve statistical instability. This work provides a verified baseline and a cautionary framework for statistical reporting in HAR, emphasizing protocol rigor over architectural complexity.

## 1. Introduction
Human Activity Recognition (HAR) has become a cornerstone technology for ubiquitous computing, enabling applications ranging from elderly care monitoring to fitness tracking and industrial safety systems [rahmani2021machine]. As these systems transition from laboratory settings to real-world deployment, the computational efficiency of the underlying models becomes a critical constraint. Edge devices, such as smartphones and wearable sensors, often lack the processing power and battery capacity to support complex Deep Learning (DL) architectures, creating a strong demand for lightweight, interpretable models that can operate within strict resource budgets [novac2021quantization, shakerian2023realtime]. The concept of "lightweight real link acceptance" posits that a model is only viable for deployment if it not only achieves high accuracy but also demonstrates proven reliability under the most rigorous evaluation protocols, ensuring that performance gains are not artifacts of data leakage or random variance.

Despite the abundance of HAR literature, a significant reproducibility gap persists regarding the evaluation of baseline models. The UCI-HAR dataset, often treated as the "MNIST of HAR," has been the subject of hundreds of studies, yet many recent works prioritize reporting raw top-1 accuracy on increasingly complex architectures while neglecting the statistical rigor required to validate these claims [mekruksavanich2023hybrid, yuan2024bstcahar]. A pervasive issue is the inconsistent application of subject-independent splits; numerous studies inadvertently introduce data leakage by normalizing features using global statistics or by failing to strictly separate training and testing subjects, thereby inflating performance metrics [chen2024private, qin2022domain]. Furthermore, the reliance on single-seed experiments or insufficient repetition (e.g., $n=2$) prevents the calculation of meaningful confidence intervals, making it impossible to distinguish between genuine architectural improvements and statistical noise [stojchevska2023real]. This lack of rigor has led to a "depth bias," where complex models are assumed superior without statistical evidence that they outperform simpler, linear baselines under fair conditions.

To address these challenges, we conduct a strict reproducibility audit of the UCI-HAR benchmark, focusing on the performance of linear and ensemble baselines under the most stringent evaluation constraints. Unlike previous studies that propose novel architectures or feature engineering techniques, our work treats the evaluation protocol itself as the primary variable of interest. We implement a "statistical control" framework that enforces the official subject-level train/test split without any reshuffling, utilizes three independent random seeds to estimate variance, and applies both parametric and non-parametric paired statistical tests. We evaluate two canonical models: a Linear Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) classifier with logistic loss and a Random Forest ensemble. By adhering to a protocol that eliminates subject-level leakage and rigorously quantifies uncertainty, we aim to establish a definitive performance floor for lightweight models.

The contributions of this work are threefold:
*   **Rigorous Baseline Verification**: We provide a code-verified reproduction of Linear SGD and Random Forest on UCI-HAR, reporting mean Accuracy and Macro-F1 scores with 95% confidence intervals under the official subject split.
