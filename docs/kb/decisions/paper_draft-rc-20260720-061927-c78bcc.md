# Stage 20: Paper Draft

## UCI-HAR Baseline Audit: Reproducibility of Linear SGD and Random Forest under Strict Subject Splits

## Abstract
Human Activity Recognition (HAR) benchmarks often obscure subject-level data leakage and lack rigorous statistical validation, leading to inflated performance claims for lightweight models. While deep learning dominates recent literature, the true upper bound of classical linear and ensemble methods under strict subject-independent protocols remains ambiguous. This study presents a reproducibility audit of Linear Stochastic Gradient Descent (LogLoss) and Random Forest (200 estimators) on the official UCI-HAR dataset. We enforce the original subject-based train/test split, utilize three independent random seeds, and report Accuracy and Macro-F1 with 95% confidence intervals. Our results indicate that Linear SGD achieves a mean Macro-F1 of 0.937, slightly outperforming Random Forest at 0.928. However, paired statistical tests yield conflicting significance levels (paired t-test $p=0.007$ vs. Wilcoxon signed-rank $p=0.25$), highlighting the instability of significance testing with limited sample sizes. These findings suggest that while linear models are highly competitive under strict protocols, definitive superiority claims require expanded sampling to resolve statistical ambiguity. This audit provides a verified baseline and a cautionary framework for statistical reporting in HAR.

## 1. Introduction

Human Activity Recognition (HAR) has become a cornerstone of ubiquitous computing, enabling applications ranging from elderly care monitoring to personalized fitness tracking [rahmani2021machine]. As these systems transition from controlled laboratory settings to real-world deployment, the demand for lightweight models capable of operating on resource-constrained edge devices has intensified [novac2021quantization, lim2023efficient]. The "lightweight link acceptance" paradigm posits that simple, interpretable models should be rigorously validated as the primary baseline before deploying complex deep learning architectures. However, the current literature suffers from a reproducibility crisis where the performance of these baseline models is often reported without strict adherence to dataset protocols or statistical rigor [demrozi2021bhar].

A critical gap exists in the evaluation of classical machine learning models on the UCI-HAR dataset, often regarded as the "MNIST" of human activity recognition. While early studies established the utility of Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Random Forests [tahir2022stochastic], recent trends have shifted almost exclusively toward complex Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) [mekruksavanich2023hybrid, khatun2022deep]. This shift has inadvertently obscured the true performance ceiling of linear and ensemble methods. Many contemporary studies report marginal accuracy gains over baselines without accounting for subject-level data leakage, where information from test subjects inadvertently leaks into the training phase during feature normalization or windowing [chen2024private]. Furthermore, the reliance on single-seed evaluations or insufficient statistical testing (e.g., reporting only mean accuracy without confidence intervals) makes it difficult to distinguish genuine architectural improvements from random variance [yuan2024bstcahar].

To address these issues, we conduct a strict reproducibility audit of two canonical baselines: Linear Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) and Random Forest. Unlike studies proposing novel architectures, our contribution lies in the rigorous enforcement of the official UCI-HAR subject split and the application of robust statistical validation. We utilize three independent random seeds to estimate variance, compute 95% confidence intervals for both Accuracy and Macro-F1, and perform paired statistical tests to assess significance. This approach allows us to isolate the effect of the evaluation protocol from model complexity.

The primary contributions of this work are:
*   **Strict Protocol Enforcement**: We implement a verified pipeline that adheres strictly to the official subject-based train/test split of UCI-HAR, ensuring zero subject overlap and preventing data leakage during preprocessing.
*   **Comprehensive Baseline Audit**: We provide the first side-by-side comparison of Linear SGD and Random Forest on UCI-HAR with full statistical reporting, including 95% confidence intervals and paired significance tests.
*   **Statistical Ambiguity Analysis**: We document and analyze the conflicting results between parametric (t-test) and non-parametric (Wilcoxon) tests arising from limited sample sizes ($n=3$), offering a cautionary note for future HAR research regarding statistical power.
*   **Reproducibility Artifact**: We release the complete experimental pipeline, ensuring that all hyperparameters, random seeds, and data processing steps are fully transparent and reproducible.
