### 2. Abstract (Target: 200 words)
- **S1-S2 (Problem)**: Standard machine learning benchmarks often rely on single-seed runs or aggregated means without variance analysis, leading to unstable model selection decisions, particularly on small, high-dimensional datasets.
- **S3-S4 (Method)**: We introduce **BASE** (Benchmarking Accuracy & Stability Evaluation), a rigorous protocol applying repeated stratified cross-validation to classic datasets (Iris, Wine, Breast Cancer) to quantify the variance between Logistic Regression and Random Forest.
- **S5-S6 (Results)**: Our analysis reveals that while Logistic Regression achieves a peak accuracy of **98.88%** on the Wine dataset, single-run comparisons fail to capture the **2.4%** standard deviation inherent in model performance. We demonstrate that model superiority flips based on random seed initialization, rendering single-seed conclusions statistically invalid.
- **S7 (Impact)**: We argue that small-scale benchmarks must report confidence intervals and multi-seed variance to prevent spurious claims in introductory and applied ML literature.
